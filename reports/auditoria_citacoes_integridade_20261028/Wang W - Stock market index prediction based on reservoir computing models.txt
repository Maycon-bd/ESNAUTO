ABSTRACT  
Prediction of the financial market price is critical for financial decision-making and market policy-making. 
Recently, various machine learning and deep learning methods have been adopted to predict financial mar -
kets ’ movements using historical time series of prices. However, accurate prediction of financial prices is still a 
long-standing challenge that always calls for new approaches. In this study, a novel machine learning model of 
reservoir computing is developed to predict stock market indices. The performance of the proposed new model is 
systematically evaluated using the time series of daily closing prices of seven major international stock market 
indices including S & P500 Index, New York Stock Exchange Composite, Dow Jones Industrial Average, Nasdaq 
Composite Index, Financial Times Stock Exchange 100 Index, Nikkei 225 Index, and Shanghai Stock Exchange 
Index between January 4, 2010, and December 31, 2018 covering 2,272 trading days. The results show that our 
model outperforms the widely used deep learning methods of long short-term memory and recurrent neural 
network in most cases. To further evaluate the predictive capability of our model, we compare our model to the 
other two newly reported deep learning methods in recent studies. Comparative results also show that our model 
is competitive to those deep learning methods in predicting stock market indices. Our study contributes to the 
literature by developing novel reservoir computing models for financial market predictions. Meanwhile, our 
results also provide practical implications for financial practitioners of potential financial applications of 
reservoir computing in financial time series analysis and predictions.   
1. Introduction 
Prediction of stock price movements is essential for investment 
decision-making, portfolio management, and risk management. Though 
researchers and practitioners have made great efforts to understand the 
price dynamics ( Fama, 1995 ), and various price prediction methodolo -
gies have been developed ( Abu-Mostafa & Atiya, 1996; Zhou, min Zhou, 
Yang, & Yang, 2019; Baek & Kim, 2018; Chong, Han, & Park, 2017; 
Weng, Lu, Wang, Megahed, & Martinez, 2018 ), successful stock market 
prediction remains as one of the most challenging problems in the 
studies of financial markets ( Abu-Mostafa & Atiya, 1996 ) for the chaotic 
nonlinearity and volatility ( Yu & Yan, 2020; Rezaei, Faaljou, & Man -
sourfar, 2020 ). 
In traditional financial economics, econometrics provides statistical 
predictive models such as autoregressive moving average model 
(ARMA) ( Rounaghi & Zadeh, 2016; Rojas et al., 2008 ) and autore -
gressive integrated moving average model (ARIMA) ( Ariyo, Adewumi, & 
Ayo, 2014; Babu & Reddy, 2014; Banerjee, 2014 ). In the past few years, 
machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) have achieved significant 
developments in various domains ( Silver et al., 2016; LeCun, Bengio, & 
Hinton, 2015 ). In finance, there is a growing enthusiasm in utilizing 
techniques of machine learning in prediction of stock price movements 
such as K-nearest neighbor ( Qian & Rasheed, 2007 ), decision tree ( Qian 
& Rasheed, 2007 ), support vector machine ( Trafalis & Ince, 2000, 2005, 
2007, Patel, Shah, Thakkar, & Kotecha, 2015b, 2015a ), random forest 
( Patel et al., 2015b; Patel, Shah, Thakkar, & Kotecha, 2015a; Ballings, 
den Poel, Hespeels, & Gryp, 2015; Zhang, Cui, Xu, Li, & Li, 2018; 
Cervell ´o-Royo & Guijarro, 2020 ), Naïve-Bayes ( Patel et al., 2015a ), and

structures such as artificial neural network (ANN) ( Kim & Lee, 2004; 
Chang, Liu, Lin, Fan, & Ng, 2009; Dutta, Jha, Laha, & Mohan, 2006; 
Qian & Rasheed, 2007; Patel et al., 2015a; Zhang & Wu, 2009 ), recur -

Zhang, Yan, & Aasma, 2020 ) are proposed and demonstrate various 
capabilities in the prediction of financial markets. More recently, in -
vestigations on the activation functions of DL models reveal that recti -
fied linear unit (ReLU) works better in the prediction of multiple markets 
and various frequencies ( Orimoloye, Sung, Ma, & Johnson, 2020 ). 
Rather than directly predicting stock price itself, some studies focus on 
classifications of the stock market trend using predictive models ( Zhang 
et al., 2018 ). Most studies target the prediction of daily prices. There are 
also study reports that novel ML and DL predictive models also work for 
intraday price time series ( Ar ´evalo, García, Guijarro, & Peris, 2017 ) and 
challenging long-term market trend prediction ( Liu, Gong, Yang, & 
Chen, 2020; Cervell ´o-Royo & Guijarro, 2020 ). Instead of using price 
time series as direct input to the predictive models, some studies first 
build traditional technical indicators from the time series and conduct 
feature selections to reduce dimension before input into deep generative 
models ( Haq, Zeb, Lei, & Zhang, 2021 ) or hybrid fuzzy neural networks 
( Garcia, Guijarro, Oliver, & Tamo ˇsi ¯unien ˙e, 2018 ). In another approach, 
neural network-based feature extractors are proposed to predict stock 
market indices ( Lee & Kim, 2020 ). There is a trend to combine DL or 
LSTM models with other methods such as knowledge graph ( Long, Chen, 
He, Wu, & Ren, 2020 ), frequency spectra decomposition models ( Rezaei 
et al., 2020 ), phase-space reconstruction ( Yu & Yan, 2020 ), error 
correction model ( Niu, Wang, Lu, Yang, & Du, 2020 ) to enhance the 
overall predictive power. Meanwhile, DL can also be used to develop 
predictive models based on market sentiment analysis ( Li, Bu, Li, & Wu, 
2020 ) and trader emotions ( Chun, Ahn, Kim, & Lee, 2020 ). There is no 
doubt that with successful stock market predictive models, it would be 
straightforward to build potential trading strategies ( Ar ´evalo et al., 
2017; Zhang et al., 2020; Lee & Kim, 2020 ). 
However, deep learning algorithms usually involve a staggering 
amount of hyper-parameters, especially when they have multiple layers 
and many neurons. This often makes the training of a layered deep 
learning algorithm costly and time-consuming. In another approach, the 
echo state network (ESN) is proposed by utilizing a sparsely connected 
hidden layer in which training the whole neural network is waived 
( Jaeger & Haas, 2004 ). Almost simultaneously, liquid state machines 
(LSM), which share the same strategy of ESN, are proposed ( Maass, 
Natschl ¨ager, & Markram, 2002 ). ESN and LSM are suitable in time series 
analysis such as blast furnace gas ( Sheng, Zhao, Liu, & Wang, 2012 ), 
financial markets ( Lin, Yang, & Song, 2009 ), and object trajectories in 
robotics ( Burgsteiner, Kroll, Leopold, & Steinbauer, 2007 ). In this line of 
ESN and LSM studies, a general novel structure called reservoir 
computing (RC) is introduced ( Luko ˇsevi ˇcius & Jaeger, 2009; Luko -
ˇsevi ˇcius, Jaeger, & Schrauwen, 2012; Verstraeten, Schrauwen, D ’ Haene, 
& Stroobandt, 2007; Appeltant et al., 2011 ). In a typical RC, a set of 
nodes in a directed random network serves as the reservoir in which 
nodes update their states based on the incoming connections and their 
previous states. Inputs and outputs are connected to some random hid -
den nodes in the reservoir, respectively. In training, the input vector is 
projected into a high-dimensional space represented by the reservoir. 
Instead of training a large number of weights in the reservoir, only 
output weights are trained using available optimization techniques. This 
approach dramatically reduces training efforts and speeds up the 
learning process. 
While much-emerging research attention has been focused on RC in 
time series ( Wyffels & Schrauwen, 2010; Butcher, Verstraeten, Schrau -
wen, Day, & Haycock, 2013 ), systematic investigations of applying RC in 
financial market predictions are still insufficient, compared to the rich 
literature using machine learning or deep learning. Especially, system -
atic evaluations of reservoirs in different topologies on multiple inter -
national markets in substantial time ranges have not been reported yet. 
To contribute to the study of RC in financial time series prediction, an RC 
model is proposed in this study. Empirical evaluations have been con -
ducted on seven major international stock markets ’ daily indices span -
ning over nine years of 15,723 data points. We systematically 
investigated three types of network topological structures, namely the 
Erd ˝os-R ´enyi random network (ER) ( Erd ˝os & R ´enyi, 1960 ), Small-World 
network (SW) ( Watts & Strogatz, 1998 ), and Scale-Free network (SF) 
( Barab ´asi & Albert, 1999 ). In comparative experiments, we find that our 
RC approach outperforms of LSTM ( Kim & Won, 2018; Baek & Kim, 
2018 ) and RNN ( Rather, Agarwal, & Sastry, 2015; Qin et al., 2017 ) in 
predictions of some stock market indices. Additionally, we report that 
our RC also outperforms two newly proposed deep learning methods 
reported in recent literature using the same stock market indices data -
sets ( Baek & Kim, 2018; Zhou et al., 2019 ).

introduction to RC in Section 2 . Then we describe our proposed RC 
model as well as the datasets of seven international major stock markets

2. Architecture of reservoir computing 
Training the connectivity weight matrices is computationally 
expensive for most deep learning models ( LeCun et al., 2015 ). Unlike 
these neural networks in which the weights of all layers are trained, the 
reservoir computing architecture only requires the readout layer 
weights to be trained. In contrast, the input network and the reservoir 
network remain untrained. Initially introduced as Echo State Network 
(ESN) ( Jaeger, 2001 ) and Liquid State Machines (LSM) ( Maass et al., 
2002 ), reservoir computing (RC) models have demonstrated success in a 
variety of high-dimensional and temporal applications ( Schrauwen, 
Verstraeten, & Campenhout, 2007; Ib ´a ˜nez-Soria, Garcia-Ojalvo, Soria- 
Frisch, & Ruffini, 2018; Jaeger & Haas, 2004; Jalalvand, Demuynck, 
Neve, & Martens, 2018 ). Thanks to the dynamical nature of a reservoir, 
RC models have more recently been even implemented as physical res -
ervoirs ( Tanaka et al., 2019 ) using photonic systems ( Pauwels, der 
Sande, Bouwens, Haelterman, & Massar, 2018 ) or dynamic memristors 
( Du et al., 2017 ).

3.2. Data 
A stock market index is a benchmark measurement of the whole 
market performance computed from a portfolio of components which 
are usually representative stocks from different economic sectors using 
various weighting methods. Prediction of the movement of a stock 
market index is important for investment and economic policy-making. 
In this study, we collect the daily closing prices of seven leading inter -
national stock indices, including S & P500 Index (Standard & Poor ’ s), 
NYSE Composite (New York Stock Exchange), DJI (Dow Jones Industrial 
Average), Nasdaq Composite Index (Nasdaq Stock Exchange), FTSE 100 
Index (Financial Times Stock Exchange 100 Index of London Stock Ex -
change), Nikkei 225 Index (Tokyo Stock Exchange), and SSE Index 
(Shanghai Stock Exchange). These are the most followed indices repre -
senting major global economies. On the other hand, the prices of these 
indices are widely used as benchmarks in the literature on predictions of 
stock indices. Therefore, our study follows this line of literature by using 
prices of these indices, though some literature use returns instead of 
prices. The list of indices is presented in Table 1 . All prices are historical 
daily closing prices spanning nine years from January 4, 2000, to 
December 31, 2018. In total, 15,723 data points are collected from the 
public financial data services of Yahoo Finance and Investing.org. 
Table 2 shows the descriptive statistics of all indices. On average, there 
are over 2,000 trading days for each index. This allows our dataset to be 
one of the largest datasets used in literature of other machine learning 
methods as well as RC. The movements of all indices are plotted in Fig. 3 . 
As illustrated, our study period is large enough to contain various 
market situations. Though the markets are correlated in certain degrees, 
the predictions of these chaotic markets are conducted as independent 
tasks. The training and test periods are colored in green and yellow, 
respectively. 
4. Experiments and results

4.3.3. Impacts of RC networks and settings 
To investigate how the topological structure impacts the perfor -
mance of a reservoir model, we generated reservoirs in three types of 
networks, namely random network (ER), scale-free network (SF), and 
small-world network (SW). As shown in Table 8 , the best performing 
structure is SF for predictions of DJI, FTSE, and SSE, SW for NYSE, 
NASDAQ and N225, and ER for S & P500, respectively. These networks 
are also in different sizes ranging from 10 to 100. This shows that the 
best performances could be achieved for different index predictions by 
different structures with different sizes. No single structure with a 
certain size can perform well in all cases. Furthermore, as illustrated in 
Fig. 7 , the three types of structures perform close to each other in the 
prediction of the same index. However, the metrics reported in the 
Table 8 show that there are still significant differences for different 
combinations of structures, sizes, lengths of input windows, and learning 
rates in the training of output weights. The best performances are ach -
ieved when the learning rate is 0.1 in predictions of S & P500, while the 
learning rate is 0.001 for the rest indices. Larger learning rates tend to 
speed up the learning process but might not even converge. From the 
results, we see that lower learning rates are acceptable, since our main 
objective is to achieve best predictions. Different sizes of input windows 
are evaluated to see what is the optimal input size of historical trading 
data for each index. The results show that RC achieves best perfor -
mances when the input is set to an optimal length, which is 5 for NAS -
DAQ, 15 for S & P500, and 20 for NYSE, DJI, FTSE, N225, and SSE, 
respectively. In the financial industry, the five trading days is a calendar 
week, 15 trading days is about half of calendar month, and 20 trading 
days is roughly a calendar month. Our extensive experiments hint that a 
large input window of 20 trading days is sufficient, and small input 
windows might not increase the performances in most predictions. The 
results also tell us that a systematic investigation of different combina -
tions of determinant factors, including the topological structures, sizes, 
learning rates, and lengths of input windows, is necessary to achieve 
good predictions. 
5. Conclusion 
Applying innovative machine learning techniques to predict move -
ments of financial markets using price time series has important impli -
cations for the financial industry ( Bose & Mahapatra, 2001 ). More 
recently, machine learning and deep learning models such as LSTM and 
RNN have been investigated in various stock markets ( Selvin, Vinaya -
kumar, Gopalakrishnan, Menon, & Soman, 2017 ). Reservoir computing 
(RC) is proposed as a special RNN model. A set of randomly connected 
state nodes provides the nonlinearity to project the inputs into a high- 
dimensional space in the model. Instead of training all weights in 
traditional methods, RC only requires the output layer weights to be 
trained while keeping the input layer ’ s weights and the reservoir 
network fixed. This approach significantly simplifies training. Thanks to 
the nonlinear memory nature of the reservoir network, RC models have 
been applied in predictions of time series ( Butcher et al., 2013; Wyffels 
& Schrauwen, 2010; Ferreira & Ludermir, 2008; Escalona-Mor ´an,

Soriano, García-Prieto, Fischer, & Mirasso, 2014 ). As a novel model, 
applications of RC in financial markets are still far less explored 
compared to other well-studied machine learning or deep learning 
models such as LSTM and RNN. 
In this work, we develop RC models using the random network, 
small-world network, and scale-free network and systematically inves -
tigate the predictive capabilities in one-day ahead predictions of seven 
international major stock market indices with comparisons to LSTM and 
RNN. The seven market indices of S & P500 Index, NYSE Composite, DJI, 
Nasdaq Composite Index, FTSE 100 Index, Nikkei 225 Index, and SSE 
Index are representatives of the most followed global financial markets. 
We collect the time series of daily closing prices between January 4, 
2010, and December 28, 2018, covering 2000 trading days for each 
index. We train and test the models over the first 80% (1600 days) and 
over the rest 20% (400 days), respectively. Metrics of MSE , MAE , R
2
,
RMSE , and MAPE are evaluated for each model. We find that RC out -
performs LSTM and RNN in six indices according to RMSE and two 
indices according to MAE . In another comparative experiment, RC 
outperforms EMD2FNN ( Zhou et al., 2019 ) in predicting NASDAQ and 
SSE, and outperforms ModAugNet ( Baek & Kim, 2018 ) in the prediction 
of S & P500. Overall, the experiments demonstrate that RC can achieve 
competitive performances in most cases compared to the state-of-the-art 
models of LSTM and RNN. 
However, this study still has limitations that need further studies. 
Firstly, the dataset only covers seven major stock market indices. 
Though this study unitizes a larger dataset with more indices and larger 
time ranges compared to other studies, it would be worth doing 
empirical tests that consider the time series of more individual stocks 
from different markets. Meanwhile, readers should be noted that these 
indices are correlated, and using a limited number of indices would 
hinder the generalization of predictive models. Though the indices are 
predicted independently, adding more indices into the study would 
significantly improve the diversity, reduce the correlations among 
indices, and lead to more reliable models with better generalization. 
Secondly, the present dataset only considers daily closing prices. One 
might be interested in investigating how RC performs in other data 
frequencies of time-scales such as hour, minute, second, or even ultra- 
high frequencies. Thirdly, this study only focuses on stock markets. 
However, it would be natural to expand the current research to other 
financial markets such as bond markets, commodity markets, money 
markets, derivatives markets, futures markets, foreign exchange mar -
kets, and interbank markets. Fourthly, considering that the objective of 
the present study is to investigate the RC models, no practical trading 
strategies are developed based on the results achieved by RC. In further 
studies, we might build trading strategies based on predictions of RC 
models and further evaluate the values of the models in backtesting 
using real financial time series. Lastly, the proposed method is only 
applied to the time series of closing prices. It would be interesting to 
further evaluate the proposed RC models in other price time series of 
open, high, low, and market time series such as returns and risks. 
Furthermore, there is a trend of combining different machine learning or 
deep learning methods together in market predictions ( Cervell ´o-Royo & 
Guijarro, 2020; Long et al., 2020; Rezaei et al., 2020; Yu & Yan, 2020; 
Niu et al., 2020 ). However, it ’ s largely unexplored to combine RC with 
existing machine learning or deep learning methods. This ensemble 
approach would be exciting and promising for future research lines. 
The prediction of financial markets is so challenging that it ’ s hard for 
a single method to achieve the best performances in predictions of all 
individual indices. As shown by our results, RC outperforms other 
methods in many cases but still can not dominate in all predictions of all 
indices. However, this does not undermine the potential value of RC in 
predictions of financial markets. A practitioner is supposed to evaluate 
all possible methods before putting them into applications wisely. We 
believe that RC is a promising candidate adding to the present machine 
learning toolbox of financial market analysis. Hopefully, these prom -
ising research directions would open wide, rich possibilities and fruitful 
study topics for scholars. These further studies of RC in predictions of 
financial time series will also inspire new tools and applications for 
financial industry practitioners.
