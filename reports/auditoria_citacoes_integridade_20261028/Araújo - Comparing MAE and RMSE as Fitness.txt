Abstract This work aims to compare Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) as fitness
functions in a genetic algorithm used to optimize the hyperparameters of an Echo State Network (ESN)
with five probabilistic distributions. We have used financial time series data extracted from the Brazilian
Stock Market. The ESN proved to be excellent for predicting time series. However, using MAE and RMSE
within the genetic algorithm was ineffective since the results did not differ significantly despite the
asymmetrical data distribution. Two of the five distributions stood out, Uniform and Student’s t, showing
low variability both with the use of RMSE and MAE, where we graphically observed that Uniform
presented better prediction using the MAE fitness.

1 Introduction
Time Series (TS) forecasting involves mathematical models and statistical tech-
niques, which can obtain patterns in data and use them to make predictions.
Therefore, Echo State Network (ESN) is quite viable, as it presents more volatile
training. However, there is an optimization problem in its hyperparameters, as
there are certain degrees of randomness in its architecture, causing variation
problems [16]. In order to optimize the hyperparameters, we use MAE and RMSE
as ﬁtness within Genetic Algorithm (GA).

With modernity, the ﬁnancial market has become increasingly complex and
volatile, characterized as a system where purchases and sales of assets and other
ﬁnancial instruments occur. The research ﬁeld in this area is critical and chal-
lenging, as it demands much eﬀort to work with TS since they have big data.
Stock forecasting signiﬁcantly aﬀects companies, individuals, and countries,
with the ability to determine the direction they may go in the future. When
dealing with countries, stock forecasting can help control economic risks and
stabilize the ﬁnancial market. Data analysis can help companies avoid future
crises in their businesses and the processing of their data. Furthermore, investors
can use stock closing data to plan for future asset purchases or protect themselves
from market risks [22].
The frequent increase in the study of regressions in the ﬁeld of statistics
aimed at forecasting stock data generated a deepening in the use of error metrics
together with the Genetic Algorithm, where they function as ﬁtness to optimize
the hyperparameters of the ESN [10] seeking more incredible accuracy. Despite
ESN not requiring almost any training, it has two disadvantages: the number of
hyperparameters to be adjusted [ 12] and the necessity to have the input data
standardized, as all Artiﬁcial Neural Networks (ANN) [14,21]. According to [7],
GA has some advantages: they do not require assuming any speciﬁc statistical
distribution of the given data; they do not require the speciﬁcation of the model
form; and they deal eﬀectively with non-stationary data. These characteristics
help to deal with the ﬁrst ESN disadvantage.
One way to determine the accuracy of a regression model is to calculate
the diﬀerence between what is observed in the TS and what is forecasted. The
error can be calculated in various ways because it can not simply add up the
errors since there are negative and positive values that end up canceling each
other out. As a result, several strategies have been adopted to avoid cancellation.
Some examples are the Mean Absolute Error (MAE), where the average error is
calculated as a module, and the Mean Squared Error (RMSE), where the values
are squared, their average calculated, and the square root is applied.
This study proposes comparing diﬀerent error measures as ﬁtness within a
genetic algorithm that contributes to optimizing the hyperparameters ﬁtted by
an ESN using diﬀerent probabilistic distributions. We have used ﬁve probability
distributions in this study: normal, uniform, Student’s t, generalized error dis-
tribution (GED), and Asymmetric Student’s t, to analyze which would be the
most accurate.
This article has been organized as follows. Section 2 presents the theoretical
foundation and a literature review on the Echo State Network, Genetic Algo-
rithm, and probabilistic distributions. Section 3 details material and methods.
Section 4 contains and discusses the results obtained. Finally, Sect. 5 draws the
conclusion and some future work.

2 Theoretical Foundation and Literature Review
Stock market forecasting is challenging for generating non-stationary TS and
has randomness. There are two hypotheses: the Eﬃcient Market Hypothesis
(EMH) asserts that price changes in the capital market can occur independently,
although several empirical studies have demonstrated that stock market predic-
tions are possible. EMH can be divided into three types (weak, semi-strong, and
strong) according to the level of reﬂection of market information [ 6].
Previous studies have employed statistical and/or machine learning tech-
niques to forecast future values [2]. We highlight some [18] compared three neu-
ral network models: time delay, recurrent and probabilistic neural networks, and
employed conjugate gradient and multi-stream extended Kalman ﬁlter training
methods for Time Delay Neural Network (TDNN) and Recurrent Neural Net-
work (RNN) for predicting stock trends. RNN showed the best performance
among the models. [5] Used a neural network, a TS fuzzy system, and a hierar-
chical fuzzy system to verify the eﬀectiveness of the hybrid model. It was found
that the use of each one proved to be viable, concluding that there was no better.
T h eE S N ,p r o p o s e db y[10], is an RNN model. It uses reservoir calculation,
where the neurons are ﬁxed, receiving random weights that will be trained at the
output. His study was only theoretical, but in 2004, it was applied in areas related
to wireless communication. As an alternative to unstable and more complex
RNN, the ESN has been increasingly improved to simplify its training. Thus, it
has a shorter and faster cycle, in addition to using many neurons, dynamizing
the network. Furthermore, it presents signiﬁcant contributions in the ﬁeld of TS
prediction, with applicability in object tracking [22].
The GAs are metaheuristic and stochastic optimization algorithms inspired
by the process of natural evolution [9]. They are used to ﬁnd near-optimal solu-
tions to optimization problems with large search spaces. The process of GA
includes operators that imitate natural genetic and evolutionary principles, such
as crossover and mutation. The prominent feature of GA is the population of
“chromosomes” and the possibility of reaching a local optimum using them [1].

3 Material and Methods
The study of TS is signiﬁcant for learning how the stock market works, the
techniques used to make forecasts, and how to use this knowledge. To do this,
we obtained the data set of the stock’s closing prices under study, processed
it, and set up our experiments using the R program language, in which some
libraries were parameterized to improve the results. GA was used to optimize
the ESN hyperparameters. In our case, each chromosome acts as a potential
solution of a hyperparameter, and it is expressed in the form of binary strings.
These chromosomes are generated randomly, and the one that provides the better
solution gets more chance to reproduce [1].
In order to optimize, it was necessary to use the GA library, with the fol-
lowing arguments: type = “binary” (binary type), nBits = 55 (chromosome
size), popSize = 10 (number of individuals in the population), population =
gabin_Population (population for binary chromosomes), pcrossover = 0.8 (pop-
ulation crossover of 80%), crossover = gabin_spCrossover (crossover between
binary chromosomes), selection = gabin_tourSelection (generates a new pop-
ulation of individuals from the current population probabilistically, according
to individual aptitudes), elitism = 1 (the best individual only), keepBest = T
(keeps the best individual), mutation = gabin_raMutation (population muta-
tion), mutation = 0.1 (mutation of 10%), maxiter = 10000 (maximum number
of epochs), run = 3500 (maximum number of unimproved epochs).
The genetic algorithm’s ﬁtness calculation used the MAE and RMSE as cri-
teria for evaluating the model, assigning 40% of the training MAE and 60% of
the validation MAE to optimization, also serving for the RMSE ﬁtness.

We divide GA processing into six steps: initialization, ﬁtness calculation, ter-
mination condition check, selection, crossover, and mutation. In the initializa-
tion stage, the generated chromosomes are selected arbitrarily and then undergo
a ﬁtness process calculated by the predeﬁned metric. The ﬁtness function is
a method used to measure the performance of this chromosome. Optimization
using a genetic algorithm requires the deﬁnition of ﬁtness. Through this function,
only the solutions with the best performance will be kept to repeat the process,
characterizing the termination condition check stage. The superior chromosomes
are reused in the selection process to reproduce better ones. This way, new solu-
tions, such as the crossover stage, will be created from existing ones. Finally, in
the mutation phase, one of the chromosomes is chosen to change a random bit.
The objective of the process is to increase the set of solutions [6].
Fig. 1. Methodology of ESN with GA for tuning hyperparameters.
Figure1 shows all the processing GA carries out with the parameters passed
in. Initially, a new chromosome is generated through selection, crossover, and
mulling, ﬁnally arriving at the stop condition. If the condition is not met, the
selection, crossover, and mulling processes are repeated, creating a superior chro-
mosome. In this study, the ﬁtness evolution used are mean absolute error (MAE)
and root-mean-squared error (RMSE).
This conﬁguration in the GA reﬁnes the hyperparameters returned by the
ESN in the statistical distributions used. The ESN hyperparameters used in this
work are ﬁve: Leaking rate ( α), Spectral radius (sr), Disregarded Initial Values
(DIV), Reservoir size, and Regularization coeﬃcient. Respectively, the leaking
rate controls the speed with which the reservoir state is updated in response to
new input weights, which are memories. The second is responsible for the sta-
bility of the neural network; if the spectral radius of the weightsW
r within the
reservoir is less than 1, ESN will have an echo state property. The reservoir size
refers to the modeling capacity and performance of the network. A larger size
means a more accurate stock forecast, but there will be an overﬁtting problem,
while too small a size can lead to underﬁtting. Finally, the regularization coef-
ﬁcient helps to avoid overﬁtting the training data, balancing training accuracy
and network compactness [24].

5 Final Considerations
In this work, we used MAE and RMSE as the ﬁtness of a genetic algorithm to
optimize the hyperparameters of an ESN and compared the test results obtained.
The use of ESN showed that it is an excellent machine learning technique
for forecasting TS, as the validation, training, and testing stages had very close
values. We observed that the accuracy measured by both MAE and RMSE, the
minimum average errors were less than 1% in all cases.
The change of MAE by RMSE within GA was not very eﬀective since the
data obtained did not diﬀer signiﬁcantly. Therefore, either MAE or RMSE can
be used. However, two of the ﬁve distributions had the lowest test and variability
values, namely uniform and GED. In this case, using GED with RMSE ﬁtness
had the best MAE and RMSE test results.
