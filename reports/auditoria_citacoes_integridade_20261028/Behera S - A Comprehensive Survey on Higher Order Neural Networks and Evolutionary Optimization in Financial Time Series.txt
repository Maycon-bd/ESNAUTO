SURVEY ARTICLE
A Comprehensive Survey on Higher Order Neural Networks 
and Evolutionary Optimization Learning Algorithms in Financial Time 
Series Forecasting

Received: 27 February 2023 / Accepted: 5 May 2023 / Published online: 23 May 2023

Abstract
The financial market volatility has been a focus of study for experts over past decades. While stockbrokers and investors 
expect reliable projections of future stock indices, it instead displays unpredictable, complicated, and nonlinear reactions 
which paves path towards designing accurate prediction mechanisms for approximate stock market behaviour. Many com-
putational intelligence methods have been used in the field of economic forecasting alongside more conventional methods. 
Yet, a trustworthy forecasting depends heavily on the correct selection of an efficient model and optimum set of model 
hyperparameters. In this regard, artificial neural networks (ANNs) specifically higher-order neural networks (HONNs) have 
shown notable performance in modelling the chaotic behaviour of financial data and predicting future data. Improved preci-
sion and nonlinear decision boundaries are two benefits of using HONN. They can store more data, map features beautifully 
with weights that are configurable on a single layer, learn quickly, and even tackle complex real-world situations. Besides, 
evolutionary optimization algorithms (EOA) are widely employed for adjusting HONN parameters thus, enhancing their 
generalization capability. HONNs are trained with several flavours of evolutionary learning algorithms to discover optimal 
solutions for various real-world complex engineering problems. The purpose of this article is to explore HONN and EOA 
based financial forecasts from the last two decades and see how well these forecasts performed in terms of making accurate 
and reliable financial predictions. We conducted a rigorous survey on state-of-the-art HONN and EOA based methods used 
for financial forecasting collected from reliable and reputed sources, analysed their applicability and performability, identi-
fied their strength and limitations, and suggested few constructive criticisms. Findings of this study may assist researchers 
of this field in adopting such forecasting methods appropriately.
1 Introduction
Everything that can be banked, invested in the stock market,

financial market. Moreover, the stock market's traces have 
come to represent many financial characteristics; therefore,

The term “trading” refers to the practise of purchasing and 
selling financial products such as stocks, equities, commod-
ities, futures, etc. Due to the unpredictable nature of the 
stock market, investors are exposed to a potentially disas-
trous environment in which to put their money. If you're an 
investor, you need to do your homework before making any 
commitments. Researchers, brokers, investors, and financial 
institutions are all interested in developing methodologies 
for accurate stock market prediction because of the stock 
market’s notoriously erratic and unpredictable nature. By 
extrapolating the current stock price and anticipated stock 
movement, analysts may estimate what the market will look 
like in the future and the dangers that come with it. A stock 
market trend is the general upward or downward price move-
ment of a stock. It is quite challenging to mine the securi-
ties market trend due to the market’s intensive nonlinearity, 
high uncleanness, and non-stationary behaviour. As a result

have greater exploration and exploitation capabilities, they 
can capture the non-linearity in time series data, they have 
a high ability to determine both optimal and hyper-optimal 
parameters of the network model, etc.

1.1  Motivation
One of the most promising areas of study in the scientific 
world is artificial neural networks. Recently, a number of 
studies have been conducted by researchers on the appli-
cations of ANNs and DL techniques in various domains, 
including cryptocurrency prediction [40], stock prediction 
[28, 41, 42], exchange rate prediction [42, 43], crude oil 
[42, 44], quaternionic domain [6 ], image processing [45], 
inventory management [46], estimation of virtual power 
plants [47], time series forecasting [48], spectrum sensing 
[49–51], routing [49], airways disorder illness [52], and solar 
energy forecasting [53]. While numerous studies have shown 
success using ANNs like MLP for classification and predic-
tion, the model has been shown to have drawbacks like local 
minima, sluggish convergence, and excessive computation 
time. Researchers have shown interest in HONNs [54] to 
get around these shortcomings of MLP. HONN has been 
employed in many prediction and classification issues due 
to its many advantageous characteristics, such as its short 
learning time, good representation of input space, and great 
learning skills that require less memory in terms of loads 
and nodes. HONN have a number of appealing character -
istics, including quick learning, a large store capacity, fault 
tolerance, and superior mapping. Because of the higher-
order expressions with which it is associated, HONN is now 
capable of providing superior nonlinear boundaries and high 
classification accuracy compared to conventional linear neu-
rons. Evolutionary optimization, on the other hand, is a more 
complex strategy. Optimization entails looking for the best 
possible way to achieve a desired goal. Changes to input sig-
nals, fine-tuning of device or simulation model parameters, 
and iterative mathematical exploration are only a few of the 
numerous activities involved in the optimization process. 
Metaheuristic algorithms, of which evolutionary algorithms 
are a subset, may be further classified into two subsets: those 
that use a population-based approach and those that use a 
single population.
Additionally, we may state that evolutionary algorithms 
are metaheuristics that rely on a population. An abundance 
of metaheuristic algorithms [55] draws ideas from natural 
systems. Further, many optimization issues may be effec-
tively resolved by using evolutionary algorithms. Here, we 
undertake a comprehensive literature review of HONN-
based financial time series forecasting. The primary reasons 
we chose HONNs for this specific survey are their high qual-
ity and reliability. Our selection of an evolutionary-based 
optimization technique stems in large part from our inter -
est in investigating the efficacy of various EOAs in training 
HONNs across several dimensions. Various research studies 
have proven financial prediction using evolutionary comput-
ing. Advances in evolutionary computation and hybridiza-
tion of evolutionary approaches with ANNs especially with

2  Higher Order Neural Network (HONN)
Normally an HONN contains zero or one hidden layer and 
one changeable weight. HONN variations and operation 
methods vary. Some do summation and then multiplication, 
while others do the opposite. HONN quickly learns non-
linearity due to a single layer of teachable loads. Higher 
words boost the HONN’s representational power in solving 
complicated problems and information capacity. HONNs are 
excellent for researchers due to their simple structure and 
high computing ability.
2.1  Variants of Higher Order Neural Network
Considering the presence or absence of hidden layer, 
order of operations, input signal expansions etc. HONN 
got different variants. Some of the well known HONNs are 
PSNN, SPNN, JSPNN, FLANN, RPNN, DRPNN, SONN, 
SLFN, ELM, CPNN, ESN, SMNM, RBFN, FLN and name 
a few. Due to simple structure, it is easy to get a variant 
of any HONN for better results. Some of the frequent use 
HONN models are briefly covered in this section.
2.1.1  Pi‑Sigma Neural Network (PSNN)
A PSNN has three layers. Between the input and middle 
layers is a weight set. The intermediate layer is a concealed 
unit that performs summing, although its load is unity 
[54]. Calculation layer calculates model output. Weighted 
inputs are connected to the hidden layer, which performs 
summing, and the result passes to the output layer’s prod-
uct unit. Only one adjustable hidden layer reduces this 
network’s learning time. This network’s product unit gives 
higher order capabilities and stronger nonlinear separa-
bility through exploration without exponential growth of 
weights. Figure  4 depicts the basic layout of a PSNN for 
prediction.
The product of the weighted input signal between the ith 
input unit and the jth hidden unit is the output of the jth 
summation unit, as shown in Eq.  1.

2.2  Applicability of HONNs for Financial Time Series 
Forecasting
Our study concentrates on the exceptional performance of 
HONNs in financial market prediction, although they are 
also used in many other financial applications. HONNs and 
their variants are heavily used for financial time series pre-
dictions because of their low complexity structure, faster 
learning properties, and better generalization power, and 
they are found to be more reliable and stable models than 
other ANN models. To invest profits, traders may need a reli-
able prediction strategy and clear laws. Building a reliable 
prediction model is tough due to non-linearity and market 
volatility. A reliable financial prediction model is error-toler-
ant, capable of capturing market anomalies and uncertainty, 
and produces high accuracy by minimizing or completely 
eliminating mistakes. Another trait of a reliable prediction 
model is its capacity to adapt to any training procedure in 
order to get the best outcome.

2.3  Analytical Discussion
It is clear from the reviewed literature (from Table  1) that 
HONN and its variants are widely used in financial pre-
diction, such as stock index prediction, bitcoin price pre-
diction, wind energy forecasting, function regularization, 
classification, electricity consumption, and many more. 
High accuracy, rapid convergence, and low error rates in 
financial time series prediction are all hallmarks of HONN 
and its derivatives. One may see how many HONN varia-
tions are making useful contributions to financial time series 
forecasting by looking at the literature. Figure  10 gives a 
clear picture of the different kinds of HONNs that are used 
to predict financial time series.
In contrast to the HONNs, whose weights tend to grow 
exponentially with network size, the PSNN structure 
requires fewer weights while still being able to solve the pre-
diction problem quickly and accurately. It is clear that PSNN 
[14, 19, 21, 32, 33, 35, 37, 66–69, 71–90] has been used in 
the vast majority of studies. PSNN has a straightforward 
structure, a single trainable weight, and a short learning 
curve, all of which contribute to its widespread popularity. 
Our research shows that compared to other hybrid models, 
PSNN performs better in several aspects, including accu-
racy, training time, steadiness, reliability, capacity to map 
non-linearity, and convergence. PSNN is also outperform-
ing some classic predators, including MLP, MLR, and other 
traditional models. Even PSNN’s offspring, such as JPSNN 
[67, 98–101], AE-PSNN [94], RPSNN [95], PSNN-EF [96], 
ARMATPS-ANN [92, 93], EPSNN [91] and MSPSNN [98] 
are better than more traditional models. FLANN [17, 34, 67, 
69, 79, 80, 84, 102–114] is a recent development of the pro-
gress of a higher-order network that has investigated various 
effective ways to forecast the major complex financial time 
series. In light of the foregoing, it may be concluded that a 
FLANN model performs better than its competitors. It yields 
more reliable predictions, requires less computing time, 
yields meaningful statistical data, converges more quickly, 
and produces fewer errors. There was also a noticeable 
improvement in performance metrics. The FLANN model 
has also been compared to some of the more common fore-
casting models, such as the MLP, ARIMA, BPNN, SVM, 
SVR, BPRBF, and LSE, and found to be superior in terms 
of error reduction, precision, and speed of convergence, 
to name a few. Similarly, many variants of FLANN, i.e., 
HFLANN [115–117], CFLANN [55, 118, 119], LFLANN 
[55, 118, 119], PFLANN [55], TFLANN [55, 118, 119], 
LeFLANN [118, 119], TPFLANN [120], FFLANN [121], 
RCE-FLANN [122, 123], RFLANN [124], FLN [125–128], 
and MFLN [172], outperformed both FLANN and other tra-
ditional models in terms of flat network structure, low com-
plexity, fast learning, nonlinear mapping, generalization, and 
high accuracy. HONNs’ use of multivariate polynomials, 
which leads to an explosion of weights, makes them difficult 
to work with, while RPNN’s use of univariate polynomials 
[158, 161], which makes it simple, makes it a good choice.

solving more complex problems with quick learning. The 
RPNN can incorporate dynamic systems by taking input and

3  Evolutionary Optimization Algorithm 
(EOA)
Due to their population-based design and inspiration from 
natural selection and evolution, evolutionary algorithms are 
classified as a type of metaheuristic. Researchers frequently 
employ evolutionary techniques to fine-tune their models, 
functions, and methodologies in search of the best possible 
answer to a research question. Numerous researchers have 
spent a lot of time probing the waters of numerous appli-
cations of evolutionary optimization techniques, including 
but not limited to forecasting, classification, optimization, 
regularisation, training, prediction, etc. In every scenario, 
evolutionary optimization methods outperform their more 
conventional counterparts. Error minimization, nonlinearity 
mapping, accuracy, and other areas have all been proven to 
benefit from evolutionary approaches. Since they are based 
on observations of nature, new evolutionary optimization 
methods are bound to emerge over time. In this study, we 
have gone over some of the many evolutionary optimiza-
tion methods that have made useful contributions to the 
field of monetary time series evaluation. We have reviewed 
several of the most well-known evolutionary optimization 
techniques, including GA, PSO, and DE, in this section. We 
also provided a brief elaboration of a few newly proposed 
evolutionary metaheuristics, including TLBO, CRO, BHO, 
JAYA, GWO, and FWA. By reading the academic literature, 
interested readers can learn more about the remaining EOAs.

3.1  Genetic Algorithm (GA)
Holland invented genetic algorithm in 1975 [9 ], inspired 
by Darwin’s notion of natural evolution. This algorithm 
mimics natural selection, where the best parents are cho-
sen. This algorithm shuffles individual solutions. GA ran-
domly selects current population members as parents for 
each generation. Over succeeding generations, the popu-
lation “evolves” toward an optimal solution. Prospective 
readers can study articles in literature [36, 66, 70, 94, 100, 
115, 117– 120, 126, 132, 150– 152, 164, 169, 173, 180, 
184] to learn about the extensive application of GA in 
financial time series prediction. Several improved versions 
of GA, like multi-objective GA (MOGA) [73] and hybrid 
GA (HGA) [87], have also been suggested and shown to 
be good at predicting financial time series. GA comprises 
four phases.
1. Initial Population: A population of chromosomes starts 
this process. Each chromosome solves a difficulty. Genes 
define chromosomes.
2. Selection: Based on their fitness levels, two sets of chro-
mosomes (parents) are chosen. Fitter chromosomes are 
more likely to be passed on to future generations.
3. Crossover: In a GA algorithm, this operator is the most 
crucial phase. A crossover point is chosen at random 
from the genes for each mating pair. The creation of a 
new organism depends on the transfer of DNA from one 
set of parents to another. The crossover operator is used 
to enhance algorithmic exploitation. This probe actually 
probes the area around a chromosome.
4. Mutation: Some of the genes in a newly produced off-
spring can be mutated. Improve exploration with the 
help of the mutation operator.
One of the key steps in GA modelling is defining a 
solution in the form of a chromosome. A chromosome, as 
defined in GA, a single-point crossover operation, and GA’s 
mutation procedure is shown below in Fig.  13.
3.2  Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)
One of the most influential intelligent optimization tech-
niques in swarm intelligence is the PSO algorithm. The 
PSO technique was proposed and initially developed by 
Russell C. Eberhart and James Kennedy in the year 1995 
[10] in order to imitate animal social interactions (e.g., a 
flock of birds searching for food). This algorithm mimics 
how members communicate and exchange information. 
Numerous optimisation problems have been solved using 
particle swarm optimization, both alone and in conjunction 
with other current techniques. Through agents, sometimes 
known as particles, whose trajectories are modified by a 
stochastic and a deterministic component, this technique 
searches for the best possible solution. The group’s “best” 
position and each particle “best” attained position impact 
it, yet particles move haphazardly overall. For application 
of PSO in financial time series prediction interested readers

3.3  Differential Evolution (DE)
DE was proposed by Storn and Price in 1995 [188]. DE uses 
straightforward formulas to combine current candidate solu-
tions to create new candidate solutions, maintain a popula-
tion of candidate solutions, and keep the candidate solution 
with the highest score or fitness on the optimization task at 
hand. In this method, the gradient is not required since the 
optimization issue is viewed as a “black box” that only offers 
a measure of quality given a candidate solution.
Various applications of DE in financial time series predic-
tion are given in the literature [37, 74, 85, 88, 91, 109, 123, 
140, 167, 178]. The performance of a hybrid DE learning 
method known as “shuffled DE” (SDE) can be seen in [68]. 
Figure  15’s flowchart explains the DE algorithm’s funda-
mental operating concept.
3.4  Teaching–Learning Based Optimization (TLBO)
Rao et al. [15] introduced a metaheuristics technique called 
teaching–learning-based optimization (TLBO). The TLBO 
algorithm is a population-based method to problem solv -
ing that replicates the teaching and learning processes in a 
classroom. TLBO is divided into two phases: the instructor 
phase and the learner phase. The learner with the highest 
grade in the population is chosen to be the teacher during 
the teacher phase. The instructor is in charge of instructing 
the students and raising the class’s average grade. During 
the learner phase, each learner chooses a learner at random 
to interact with. At this stage, the ultimate aim is to raise the 
class’s mean grade. The above operations are iterated until 
the halting condition is reached.
The interested reader might read Zou et al., [189] for 
a summary of the most current TLBO improvements.

4  Hybridization of EOAs and HONNs 
and Financial Time Series Forecasting
To increase the prediction accuracy of ANNs, especially 
HONNs, EOAs are heavily used to regulate their parameters. 
It was discovered that they were superior to gradient descent 
learning in several areas, including their capacity for global 
minima, quick convergence, and flexibility.
A genetic algorithm (GA) was used by Nayak et al. [66]

Dash et al. [32] used a crow search algorithm (CSA) to train

models.

4.1  Technical Discussion
According to the findings of our study (which can be found 
in Table  2), a variety of evolutionary optimization tech-
niques, in particular those utilized in the process of pre-
dicting financial time series, produce reliable results. A pie 
chart illustrating the relative contribution of various different 
evolutionary algorithms to the process of financial forecast-
ing is presented for your perusal in Fig.  22. (as far as our 
survey is concerned).
The review of the relevant literature makes it abundantly 
clear that the majority of the researchers used genetic algo-
rithms (GA) [36, 66, 94, 100, 115, 117–120, 126, 132, 
150–152, 164, 169, 173, 180] for accurate financial time 
series forecasting. This is the case due to the fact that GA 
provides optimal global search, subset selection, and near-
optimal parameter search. The combination of HONN and 
GA resulted in improved generalization, a more rapid con-
vergence with global optima, reduced structural complex-
ity in the network, and increased accuracy in predictions. 
To take the performance of GA one step further, certain 
upgraded versions of GA, such as multi-objective GA 
(MOGA) [73] and hybrid GA (HGA) [87], have also been 
presented and have demonstrated their effectiveness in finan-
cial time series forecasting. PSO [78, 107, 131, 138, 149, 
157, 162, 165] is a simple algorithm that may be effectively 
employed to fine-tune the parameters of a wide variety of 
HONNs due to its lack of complexity, simple parameters, 
and constraints. Authors have created variations of PSO, 
such as MPSO [81, 92, 93, 97], HMRPSO [141], RWPSO 
and WLDPSO [146], and IPSO [76, 108] to address the slow 
convergence that is sometimes seen.
In order to get better results, a hybrid learning approach 
called CRPSO was introduced in article [137]. In [134], the 
network parameters are adjusted using a combination of PSO 
and a discrete PSO (DPSO) to boost precision. Faster con-
vergence, higher accuracy, fewer control parameters, and 
efficient optimization of the many HONNs’ parameters are 
all advantages of CRO [38, 72, 77, 90, 113, 114, 124, 125, 
127, 171, 177, 181], whereas CRO-LMS [55] outperforms 
both CRO and LMS learnings due to its hybrid nature. FWA 
[33, 34] was found to have faster convergence, the ability 
to find global minima, faster learning, and more accurate 
predictions than GA, PSO, and GD learning. Improved 
accuracy in prediction is one of the many benefits of the 
random search capacity, adaptive stability, and dependable 
predictability provided by FFA [21, 122, 196]. In contrast, 
DE [37, 74, 85, 88, 91, 109, 123, 140, 167, 178] is superior 
to BP, PSO, and HMRPSO when it comes to optimizing 
the parameters of different HONN because of its ease of 
use, small parameter set, rapid convergence, and flexibility. 
Furthermore, a hybrid learning technique known as SDE 
[68] is effective in locating the best possible solution at a 
fast convergence rate. With the ABC and DE presented in 
[178] and a hybrid learning approach ABC-DE presented in 
[170], there is improved prediction accuracy, greater gener-
alization, a shorter training period, a more balanced search 
strategy, optimized weights and biases, and shorter training 
periods.
Additionally, there is a more balanced search strategy. 
While ACO [178, 198] and a hybrid ACO called genetic 
ant colony algorithm (GACA) [199] provides a forecast 
that is both more accurate and ideal, A GWO [155], on 
the other hand, was able to provide superior ideal out-
comes [155]. CSA makes it possible to handle effectively 
any and all critical parameters [32]. When compared to 
CSA, hybrid learning strategies such as CMCSA [147] 
and PGCSA [168] are more capable of determining the 
parameters of the HONN like the ELM in order to con-
struct hybrid models for financial time series forecasting. 
It has been discovered that the Rao algorithm, also known 
as RA, is very effective in searching for the optimal param-
eters of FLANN [102] and ANN [194] in order to deliver 
the ideal solution for financial time series forecasting. Bet-
ter results were achieved by RA in terms of prediction 
accuracy, computation time, statistical significance testing, 
and the lowest values for loss functions such as MAPE 
and ARV values. When compared to BP and DE learn-
ing, the error function values obtained using an HS [ 17] 
were superior. By doing away with the HS’s two constant 
parameters (bandwidth and pitch adjustment rate), a new 
variation of the HS known as self-adaptive harmony search 
(SAHS) [104] has been created to boost the algorithm’s 
performance. SADHS-OELM [ 130] is a hybrid learning 
method that has been modified to improve upon the per -
formance of DE, SADHS, and other similar methods by 
optimizing both the optimum parameters and the network 
structure (hyper parameters) of a two-hidden-layer feed-
forward neural network (SLFN).
On the other hand, ISFL [71, 154] offers advantages over 
SFL, PSO, and DE in terms of learning speed, optimality 
of solutions, and global and local exploration. TLBO [14] 
is parameter less. TLBO improves accuracy. Opposition-
based learning with Elitism TLBO (OLE-TLBO) [ 103] 
outperformed GA, PSO, TLBO, E-TLBO, and OL-TLBO 
in parameter optimization. HBMO [75] calculated PSNN 
parameters better than GA, PSO, and DE. HBMO-GDL ver-
sion [105] enhanced accuracy and was found superior to 
GA, PSO, and HBMO. ABC successfully optimizes PSNN 
parameters [19] for higher accuracy. An improved ABC-DE 
[175] outperformed DE, PSO, and ABC to train the ELM 
network. Similarly, other EOAs like COA [69], JAYA [35],

5  Conclusion and Future Scope
Even though it’s difficult to make accurate projections of 
financial time series, HONN has made strides in this direc-
tion. HONN and its variations perform exceptionally well 
in economic forecasting because of their high-order terms 
and relatively straightforward structure. HONN has been 
shown to be a highly effective model for financial classifi-
cation and prediction since it does not have gradient issues 
and hence does not converge to a local minimum. Over-
fitting and under-fitting are not likely to occur because 
there is just one layer hiding beneath the surface. HONNs 
are well suited to revealing the non-linearity and concealed 
elements of financial data due to their exceptional map-
ping power. With the addition of higher-order expressions, 
HONNs have proven to be effective in tackling complex 
real-world problems, especially in financial time series 
forecasting. As we've shown in our studies, HONN and 
its variants produce excellent results when used to fore -
cast financial time series. It is demonstrated that HONN 
excels in several ANN-related metrics, including accuracy, 
loss function, convergence, learning rate, and adaptability.

perform very well in comparison to conventional EOAs.

• Because of their greater versatility in determining both

• EOAs are more suited to train ANNs since they have 
greater exploration and exploitation capabilities.
• They are capable of producing better convergence.
• They are capable enough to perform global search.
• They are not affected by the local minima problem.
• They are powerful enough to detect nonlinearity in time 
series datasets.

eters. We discovered the efficacy of EOA-based HONNs in 
creating hybrid models with improved prediction perfor -

by the performance of HONNs in financial time series fore-
casting as well as by the helpful contribution of EOAs when 
hybridized with HONNs in the same forecasting domain. In 
order to better anticipate financial time series, we want to 
use a future hybrid model that combines the most effective 
HONNs with a recently suggested EOA.
In addition to finding a new EOA to train the optimum 
parameters and hyperparameters of the HONNs to predict 
the financial time series one step ahead of time, interested 
researchers may also find ways to enhance the loss function 
and deliver maximum accuracy in financial time series fore-
casting. To optimize the HONNs for predicting financial time 
series, a number of hybrid learning methods have been pre-
sented and proven to be more effective than either EOA or 
GD-based learning alone. However, the most effective hybrid 
method of education has yet to be discovered. Interested 
researchers could create novel hybrid learning by develop -
ing and deploying some other EOA-based hybrid learning to 
train HONNs for financial time series prediction. Readers and 
researchers interested in expanding this work’s future poten-
tial may find it useful to combine deep reinforcement learning 
[50] with clustering [51] and EOAs to develop a novel hybrid 
model for predicting the future value of financial time series.
